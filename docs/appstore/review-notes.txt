WHERE THE APP IS AFTER YOU LAUNCH IT

Pastry has no window and no Dock icon. It is a menu-bar app: the only thing
that appears when you launch it is a small scissors icon at the right-hand
end of the macOS menu bar. Nothing opens, nothing lands in the Dock, and it
does not appear in Command-Tab. If launching the app looks like it did
nothing, this is why.

The App Store icon is a cupcake and the menu-bar icon is a pair of scissors.
That difference is deliberate: a menu-bar icon has to be a flat monochrome
template image, so it cannot be the app icon.

WHAT IT DOES

Pastry remembers the text you copy, so you can paste something you copied
earlier without going back to find it again.

HOW TO TEST IT, IN ABOUT A MINUTE

1. Launch Pastry. Look for the scissors icon in the menu bar.
2. In any application -- TextEdit is fine -- copy two or three different
   pieces of text with Command-C.
3. Click the scissors icon. The menu lists what you copied, newest first,
   with a search field at the top that filters as you type.
4. Choose one. It is pasted into the application you were last typing in.
5. Or, without the menu: hold Shift-Command-V down and tap V repeatedly. A
   panel appears in the middle of the screen showing one clipping at a time,
   walking back through the history. Release the modifier keys and the
   clipping you stopped on is pasted.

PERMISSIONS

The first time you choose a clipping, macOS asks for Accessibility
permission. Pastry needs it to paste into the frontmost application on your
behalf, and that is the only thing it uses it for.

Declining is not a dead end. The chosen clipping still goes onto the system
clipboard; you press Command-V yourself.

NO ACCOUNT, NO NETWORK, NO EXTERNAL SERVICES

There is nothing to sign in to and no demo account to ask for. Pastry has no
accounts, no network code of any kind, no analytics, no sync and no server.
It uses no third-party services, SDKs or data providers. Nothing it stores
leaves the Mac.

Clippings an application marks as concealed -- password fields, mostly -- are
skipped and never stored.

The app behaves identically in every region. It is English-only and has no
regional content, pricing or feature differences.
